Wayfinding walk: the Ypsilanti Transit Center

September 11, 2026 · Week 2

I spent forty minutes inside and outside the Ypsilanti Transit Center on a Tuesday morning, watching how people figured out where to stand. I picked it because I have used it maybe six times and have never once been confident I was waiting in the right place.

What I did

I stood by the north entrance from 9:20 to 10:00 and wrote down every time someone stopped moving, looked around, or asked another person a question. Then I ran the task myself: I tried to work out which bay the Route 4 leaves from using only what is printed inside the building.

What I counted

Twenty-three people entered during the forty minutes. I recorded what each one did in the first fifteen seconds after coming through the door.

Twice as many people asked another passenger as asked the staffed desk, and the desk was ten feet closer to the door.

Decision points

Four places where a person has to choose and the building does not help much.

The doors
Three entrances, no indication that one of them is closer to the bays. The schedule is taped to the one most people do not use.
The bay numbers
Painted on the curb, readable only once you are standing on the curb. You cannot see them from inside.
The information desk
Staffed, helpful, and set back behind a partition. Nothing above it says what it is for.
The arrival screen
Lists routes and times but not bays, which is the only thing anyone standing there needs to know.

What someone said

I just watch where the people who look like they know what they're doing go stand, and I go stand near them.

Passenger waiting for the Route 6, 9:41am

This was the useful part of the whole morning. People are using other passengers as the wayfinding system because the building is not one.

Running the task myself

  1. Entered through the north door. Nothing directional in sight.
  2. Found the arrival screen. It gave me a time, not a place.
  3. Walked outside to read the curb numbers. Found bay 3.
  4. Went back inside because it was cold. Lost track of the time.

Four minutes, and I already knew roughly what I was looking for. Someone doing this for the first time would take longer or would give up and ask.

What I would do next. The arrival screen already has the bay number in its data. It is in the API the app uses. It is simply not displayed. That is one field, and it would answer the question that thirteen of twenty-three people walked in with.

Vocabulary from class

  • Decision point: the doors, the screen, the curb.
  • Signifier: the painted bay numbers, which only signify once you are already there.
  • Feedback: none until you see your bus, or do not.
  • Constraint: the cold, which pushes people back inside and away from the only real information.
